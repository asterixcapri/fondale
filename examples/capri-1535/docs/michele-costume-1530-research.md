# Michele costume research: Capri, circa 1530

## Decision

The rejected turnaround must not be used as a costume reference. Its overall
silhouette reads as a modern or romanticised "pirate" outfit: a three-button
waistcoat, loose trousers gathered below the knee, short ribbed socks, and
multi-eyelet ankle boots. Those details should be replaced, not merely
recoloured.

For the next turnaround, use a coherent circa-1530 Italian working outfit:

- an undyed linen shirt with a gathered neck and narrow neckband;
- a close-fitted brown wool farsetto or jerkin, fastened closely down the front
  and ending in a short skirt or tabs at the natural waist;
- compact blue-grey upper hose or plain braghe, with a small integral codpiece;
- fitted, full-calf wool calze tied below the knee and disappearing beneath the
  upper hose;
- low, broad-toed leather shoes with simple latchet fastening;
- a narrow leather belt, a soft pouch, and a small plain working knife.

This is a reconstruction, not a claim that one complete documented "Capri
worker costume" survives. The strongest exact-date source located is Lorenzo
Lotto's 1530 *Nativity / Adoration of the Shepherds*. Its official catalogue
entry explicitly identifies `casacche di panno`, white shirts with gathered
collars and cuffs, black `farsetti`, slashed `braghe`, and coloured `calze`
fastened at the knee. It also warns that these shepherds are wealthy sitters in
fashionable clothing, so Michele should borrow the garment system and remove
the velvet, slashing, bright ribbons, and display finish
([Catalogo generale dei Beni Culturali](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/Lombardia/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/2k100-00004_R03)).

## Source controls

1. **Exact-year garment vocabulary:** Lorenzo Lotto, *Nativity / Adoration of
   the Shepherds*, 1530. The state catalogue supplies both the date and the
   garment names, as well as the crucial warning that the supposedly rustic
   figures are dressed above their station
   ([Italian Ministry of Culture catalogue](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/Lombardia/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/2k100-00004_R03)).
2. **Simpler late-1520s silhouette and carried bag:** L'Ortolano, *The Adoration
   of the Shepherds*, late 1520s. The kneeling shepherd wears a plain knee-length
   belted outer garment over hose; a soft tied bag hangs from a cord at the
   waist. Background figures also show narrow lower-leg coverings and low dark
   shoes. These observations are visual inferences from the public-domain image,
   not terminology supplied by the record
   ([Metropolitan Museum of Art, 30.95.296](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437231)).
3. **Exact-year Italian silhouette check:** the 1530 Venetian woodcut of Charles
   V's entry into Bologna records many full-length contemporary figures. It is a
   court procession, not a labouring-class source, so it is useful only for the
   relationship between short upper garments, fitted legwear, and low shoes;
   its ornament and status markers must not be transferred to Michele
   ([British Museum, 1862,0208.5](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/P_1862-0208-5)).
4. **Neckline control:** Andrea del Sarto's dated 1528–29 *Portrait of a Man*
   shows only a narrow pale shirt edge at the neck beneath the outer garment,
   rather than a modern broad pointed shirt collar. The sitter is probably a
   canon, so this is evidence for the period neckline, not for Michele's social
   class
   ([Metropolitan Museum of Art, 1982.60.9](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437610)).

## Actionable turnaround brief

### Shirt

- Material and colour: unbleached or warm ivory linen, visibly lighter and
  softer than the wool layers.
- Neck: a low round gathered opening with a very narrow band, plus a short
  centre-front slit closed by one or two linen ties. A small gathered edge may
  show. Do not draw a modern spread or pointed turndown collar.
- Sleeves: roomy at the upper arm and tapering into a narrow tied or buttoned
  wrist band. For the working pose, push or bunch the sleeves irregularly to
  mid-forearm; do not form two neat modern rolled cuffs.
- No deep open V exposing the chest. The shirt opening should remain modest and
  structurally separate from the farsetto fastening.

The gathered collar and cuff are directly named in the Lotto catalogue. The
plain ties and lack of decorative ruffle are a deliberate down-classing for a
modest worker.

### Farsetto / jerkin

- Material: plain, worn walnut-brown wool cloth, optionally linen-lined. No
  velvet, embroidery, slashing, piping, or contrasting trim.
- Shape: fitted through chest and waist, with a waist seam at the natural waist.
  End it in a shallow skirt or four to eight short, broad tabs over the upper
  hose. The back should have shaped seams rather than the straight rectangular
  back of a modern waistcoat.
- Front: close with a continuous row of small cloth-covered buttons, simple
  metal hooks, or closely spaced ties from the lower neck to the waist. Do not
  use three widely spaced waistcoat buttons or a deep modern V.
- Armholes: use a simple sleeveless reconstruction with small shoulder wings so
  the linen sleeves remain readable at sprite size. The lower edge may show a
  few discreet points or ties connecting it to the upper hose.

The existence and name of the `farsetto` are secure in the exact-year Lotto
record. Its sleeveless, shoulder-winged form for this particular Caprese worker
is a practical reconstruction and remains an uncertainty; a full-sleeved
farsetto would also be defensible, but would hide the visually useful linen
sleeves.

### Upper hose, codpiece, and calze

- Replace the current loose knee trousers with compact upper hose / plain
  `braghe` in muted blue-grey wool. They may have moderate fullness at the hips
  and upper thigh, but must not balloon like later pirate breeches or hang like
  modern trousers.
- End the upper hose at or just above the knee. Keep it unslashed and
  undecorated for Michele. Show the construction as separate from the fitted
  lower-leg calze.
- Include a small central codpiece integrated into the front closure. It should
  be historically legible in the Art Master but restrained enough to collapse
  into the torso/hip silhouette at 100 pixels. Do not replace it with a modern
  fly seam.
- Use fitted dull grey-brown or dusty ochre wool calze covering the whole lower
  leg. They are cut-and-seamed cloth, not ribbed modern knit socks. Tie them just
  below the knee with narrow tapes, with the top hidden by the upper hose.
- No bare strip of calf between sock and breeches in the canonical turnaround.

Lotto's record directly supports the combination of `braghe` and knee-fastened
`calze`; the plain, unslashed version is the lower-status adaptation. The exact
cut and attachment system used on Capri is not documented by the sources
located.

### Shoes

- Low shoes ending below the ankle, in dark brown vegetable-tanned leather.
- Broad rounded or modestly squared toe, flexible-looking thin sole, and little
  or no visible heel.
- Close with one simple latchet pair tied or buckled over the instep. Avoid a
  high padded ankle, rows of metal eyelets, hiking-boot cross-lacing, a separate
  rubber-looking outsole, or a conspicuous stacked heel.

The low-shoe silhouette is visible in the late-1520s L'Ortolano and the 1530
Bologna procession. Neither source documents a specifically Caprese shoe, so
the precise latchet construction is an informed reconstruction.

### Belt and pouch

- Use a narrow dark leather belt, approximately two fingers wide in the Art
  Master, with a small forged rectangular or D-shaped buckle. Wear it at the
  natural waist, where it visually reinforces the farsetto/hose junction.
- On Michele's physical left hip, use a soft drawstring or rounded kidney-shaped
  pouch in undyed tan or dark brown leather. Suspend it from one or two loops;
  close it with a thong or a small overlapping flap.
- The pouch must sag and crease. Do not use the current rigid rectangular
  utility pouch with a modern flap buckle and heavy top-stitching.

The soft tied waist bag in L'Ortolano is the closest near-contemporary visual
control found. Scaling it down and changing cloth to plain leather is a design
choice for Michele, not direct Capri evidence.

### Working knife

- Keep the knife as a tool, not a status weapon: a straight or gently curved
  single-edged blade, a plain wood or horn handle, and no crossguard, decorated
  pommel, knuckle protection, or sword-like hilt.
- Put it in an undecorated stitched leather sheath on Michele's physical right
  hip, hanging vertically or canted slightly backward. The complete sheathed
  tool should end well above the knee and should not dominate the silhouette.
- Avoid a clip-point/Bowie profile, a long fighting dagger, metal sheath mounts,
  and visible heraldic decoration.

No securely dated, modest Caprese working knife was located in the consulted
museum records. This element is therefore a functional reconstruction based on
the user's character brief. It should remain deliberately generic until a
regional archaeological or notarial source is found.

## Palette and condition

- Shirt: warm unbleached ivory, not bright white.
- Farsetto: walnut, bark, or umber brown wool.
- Upper hose: smoky blue-grey, desaturated and slightly uneven.
- Calze: dull ochre, mushroom grey, or muted brown; avoid matching modern socks.
- Leather: two related dark browns, with wear at toe, heel, belt holes, pouch
  corners, and sheath mouth.
- Repairs may include one small darn or patch, but do not turn the outfit into a
  theatrical beggar costume. Michele is modest and working, not destitute.

## Rejection checklist for the next Art Master

Reject the next turnaround if any view contains:

- a modern pointed shirt collar or symmetrical rolled cuffs;
- a deep-V three-button waistcoat;
- trousers with a fly, pockets, or modern inseam construction;
- baggy below-knee pirate breeches without a period upper-hose/codpiece logic;
- short ribbed socks or bare calf above an ankle sock;
- high multi-eyelet lace-up work boots;
- a wide jeans belt, rigid modern belt pouch, or oversized fighting knife;
- decorative slashing, velvet, silk ribbons, or courtly trim copied from Lotto's
  high-status shepherd sitters.

## Uncertainties and next research debt

- No source located documents one complete outfit worn by a modest young man on
  Capri or in Naples in exactly 1530. North and central Italian works are being
  used as the nearest dated controls, so regional variation remains unresolved.
- Religious shepherds are not neutral ethnographic records. Lotto's catalogue
  explicitly says its shepherds are elite sitters in fashionable disguise;
  L'Ortolano may also idealise rustic dress.
- The sleeveless farsetto, the exact division between upper hose and calze, and
  the pouch and knife construction are the most reconstructive choices.
- Before final historical sign-off, consult Campanian archaeological leather
  finds, Neapolitan notarial clothing inventories, or a specialist in early
  sixteenth-century southern Italian dress. These were not located in the
  present museum-only search.
